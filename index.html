The mathematics that makes a network learn.

Deep learning does not rely on black-box heuristics. It is built upon three foundational pillars of mathematics: Linear Algebra & Geometry (matrix affine mappings and spatial contractions), Multivariable Calculus & Analysis (directional gradients, Jacobians, and chain-rule error flows), and Optimization Theory (loss surface geometry, learning rate stability criteria, and momentum physics). This visual guide develops every principle from first intuition to full mathematical rigor.

Interactive Primer: The 60-Second Neural Sandbox

Adjust parameters, watch signals flow, and step gradient descent live

Before diving into the algebra, here is what a neural network actually does in physical terms: it multiplies inputs by adjustable weights, adds a bias, passes the result through a non-linear gate, and nudges its knobs until its error shrinks.

The Closed-Loop Feedback Cycle of Learning: Information flows forward to produce predictions; error signals flow backward to compute sensitivities; gradient descent nudges parameters to reduce cost.

CALCULUS ANALYSIS · THE CHAIN RULE

Interlocking Mechanical Gears

Open Full Chapter 2 Gear Simulator →

When Gear A turns Gear B, and Gear B turns Gear C, the overall transmission ratio of C to A is the product of consecutive tooth ratios. The intermediate Gear B's tooth count appears in both the denominator of Link 1 and the numerator of Link 2, cancelling out completely!

\(\frac{dz}{dx} = \frac{dz}{dy} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = \left(-\frac{N_B}{N_C}\right) \cdot \left(-\frac{N_A}{N_B}\right) = +\frac{N_A}{N_C}\)
Ratio: dz/dx = +12/16 = +0.750 (B cancelled!)

\(\phi(z) = \max(0, z) \implies \phi'(z) = 1 \text{ if } z > 0 \text{ else } 0\)

The whole argument in eight lines

  1. A neural network is a composition of functions; its parameters are the weights and biases.
  2. A neuron computes \(z=\mathbf w\T\mathbf x+b\), then \(a=\phi(z)\).
  3. A layer computes \(z^l=W^l a^{l-1}+b^l\), then \(a^l=\phi(z^l)\).
  4. A cost function \(C\) reduces the whole network's output to one number worth minimising.
  5. Training asks one question about every parameter: how much does \(C\) change if I change it?
  6. Backpropagation answers that question for all parameters at once, by pushing the chain rule backwards.
  7. The answers assemble into a single gradient vector \(\nabla_\theta C\).
  8. Gradient descent steps against it — \(\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\nabla_\theta C\) — and the loop repeats.

How to use this

The chapters are ordered by dependency, not by difficulty. Chapter 4 is the steepest climb and chapter 6 is the payoff; skipping 4 makes 6 look like magic, which defeats the point.

Three habits make the material go down far more easily:

  • Write the shape of every object before differentiating it. Most confusion in this subject is a transpose in the wrong place, and shapes catch that immediately. The green shape check boxes throughout do this deliberately.
  • Do the derivations on paper. Reading a chain rule is not the same as running it.
  • Answer the self-checks before opening them. They sit at the end of each chapter.

Everything is static HTML, CSS and JavaScript with MathJax for typesetting. Open index.html directly or serve the folder; there is nothing to build.
